# Test studies and long observations (fictional, synthetic data only)

Four fictional studies with one long observation note each, for testing the Clinical Research Follow-up Portal.
Each note is at least 8,000 characters and below the 16,000-character limit of the note form (about 4,000 tokens).
The notes contain no digits, names, contact details, diagnoses or clinical advice, so the AI draft can pass the guardrails.

|---|---|---|
| `STD-HYD-05` | Daily Hydration Habits Study | Observational |
| `STD-SCR-06` | Screen Time and Rest Study | Observational |

## How to use

1. Log in as a coordinator and open **Studies → New study**. Create each study with the code, title and type above.
2. Open **Participants → Register participant**, choose the study and a reviewer.
3. Use a **completed** visit for that participant. (A newly scheduled visit is in the future; to add notes straight away, use an existing completed visit or reset the database and use the seed visits.)
4. Copy everything between `--- copy from here ---` and `--- copy to here ---`, paste it into **Observations**, click **Save note**, then **Generate AI draft**.
5. Log in as the assigned reviewer to approve (final text, comment and at least one follow-up action) or reject (at least one reason).

## Observation 1: Daily Hydration Habits Study (STD-HYD-05)

--- copy from here ---

Follow-up visit completed at the study site in the morning, as the participant had requested at the previous visit. The participant arrived on time and confirmed their study code at reception. Consent was re-confirmed verbally at the start of the visit and the participant said they were happy to continue taking part in the study. No changes to consent were requested.

The participant returned the paper drinks diary for the whole period since the last visit. All pages were present and in the correct order. The coordinator reviewed the diary with the participant page by page. The diary asks the participant to record each drink, the type of drink, the approximate size of the cup or glass, the time of day, and where they were when they had the drink. Entries were complete for every day, although on several evenings the participant had written the evening drinks the following morning, and had marked these entries with a small note. The coordinator thanked the participant for marking the late entries clearly.

The participant described their drinking pattern during the period. They said they drank more water on working days because they kept a refillable bottle on their desk and refilled it at the kitchen on their floor. They said that on weekends their routine was less regular and they often forgot to drink water until the afternoon. They reported drinking tea in the morning with breakfast and again in the middle of the afternoon on most days. They said they rarely drank coffee and did not drink fizzy drinks at home. They said they sometimes had a glass of juice at weekend breakfasts with their family.

The participant mentioned that the weather had been warm for part of the period and that they had noticed they felt thirstier on those days. They said they had carried a bottle with them when walking to the shops on warm afternoons. They said they had recorded these drinks in the diary when they returned home, and had sometimes forgotten to record a drink taken while out. They had added a note on those pages to say that an outdoor drink might be missing.

The coordinator asked how the participant usually measured the size of their drinks. The participant said they used the picture guide on the inside cover of the diary, which shows a small cup, a mug, a glass and a bottle. They said the guide was helpful but that their usual mug was larger than the mug in the picture, so they had written "large mug" for those drinks. The coordinator noted that the participant's usual mug is larger than the guide and recorded this in the study notes so that the entries can be interpreted correctly.

The participant said that keeping the diary had made them more aware of how little they drank on weekend mornings. They said that on a few weekends they had started taking a glass of water to the table with breakfast. The coordinator noted this observation and reminded the participant that the study observes normal habits and does not require any change.

The participant said the diary was too large to carry in a bag, which was one reason why drinks taken outside the home were sometimes missed. They asked whether a smaller pocket-sized diary could be provided for days out. The coordinator noted this request for the study team.

The participant said they sometimes forgot to complete the diary in the evening because they were tired, and that a short reminder in the evening might help. The coordinator noted this request.

The participant completed the follow-up questionnaire on the tablet during the visit. All questions were answered and the questionnaire was submitted without errors. The participant said the questions about drinking habits during the previous weeks were easier to answer this time because they had the diary to refer to.

The participant asked for the next visit to be booked in the morning, because morning appointments fit around their work. They said early in the week would suit them best. The coordinator noted this preference.

No adverse events were reported. The participant had no questions about the study procedures beyond the requests above. The coordinator explained the next steps in the study and confirmed that the participant knows how to reach the study team between visits.

At the end of the visit the participant received a new blank drinks diary and a fresh copy of the picture guide. The participant confirmed they understood how to continue completing the diary.

Additional observations recorded during the visit: the participant arrived carrying a refillable bottle and said they had brought it from work. They said they had eaten breakfast before the visit and had a cup of tea with it. They said they felt well on the day of the visit. They asked whether drinks taken with meals should be recorded separately from drinks between meals, and the coordinator confirmed that all drinks should be recorded in the same way, with the time of day, as shown in the diary instructions.

The participant described their workplace in more detail. They said the office kitchen has a water dispenser and a kettle, and that colleagues often make tea for each other in the afternoon. They said that on busy days they sometimes forgot to refill their bottle until late in the afternoon. They said they had noticed this pattern while keeping the diary and had written "busy day" in the notes box on those pages.

The participant described their weekend routine. They said they usually have a late breakfast on Saturday and Sunday, then go out to the shops or to visit family in the afternoon. They said they usually have tea when visiting family and that these drinks were recorded in the diary when they returned home. They said they rarely carry a bottle on weekends because they do not usually think about it.

The participant said they had one evening out with friends during the period, when they had several drinks over the evening, and that they had recorded these the next morning as best they could remember. They had marked these entries as approximate. The coordinator thanked them for marking the entries clearly.

The coordinator checked the diary for any unclear entries. On two pages the drink type had been written in a way that could be read two ways, and the participant clarified the correct type. The coordinator initialled each clarification and recorded that it was given by the participant during the visit. No other entries needed clarification.

The participant asked how the diary information would be used. The coordinator explained that the diary entries are linked to the study code only and that the research team looks at general patterns across participants. The participant said they were reassured by this explanation.

The participant asked how many more visits were planned in the study. The coordinator explained the remaining schedule in general terms. The participant said the schedule was manageable and that they were happy to continue.

The participant said that the instruction sheet at the front of the diary was clear, but suggested that a short list of common drinks with tick boxes could make the diary quicker to complete. The coordinator noted this suggestion for the study team.

Before leaving, the participant repeated the main instructions back to the coordinator: record every drink with the time and the cup size, mark any late entries, and bring the diary to the next visit. The coordinator confirmed that this was correct.

The coordinator noted the following for the study record: the drinks diary was complete for the period, with late evening entries clearly marked; the participant's usual mug is larger than the picture guide; drinks taken outside the home may be missing on some days; and the participant requested a smaller pocket-sized diary, evening reminders, a tick-box list of common drinks for future diaries, and morning appointments early in the week.

The visit ended in the late morning without any outstanding questions from the participant.

The coordinator also asked whether anything else had changed in the participant's routine during the period. The participant said they had started walking to work on dry mornings instead of taking the bus, and that they usually drank water when they arrived at the office. They said they had recorded these drinks in the diary as usual and had not marked the walking days separately.

## Observation 2: Screen Time and Rest Study (STD-SCR-06)

--- copy from here ---

Follow-up visit completed at the study site in the late morning. The participant arrived on time and confirmed their study code at reception. Consent was re-confirmed verbally at the start of the visit and the participant said they wished to continue in the study. No changes to consent were requested.

The participant returned the paper screen time log for the period since the last visit. The log asks the participant to record the times they used a phone, tablet, computer or television in the evening, what the screen was used for, where they were at the time, and the time they went to bed and turned off the light. The coordinator reviewed the log with the participant page by page. Most entries were complete. The log was incomplete for one weekend, when the participant went away to visit friends and forgot to take the log with them.

The participant described their evening screen use during the period. They said they usually watched television for a while after their evening meal, then used their phone in the living room to read messages and news. They said that on several evenings they had taken their phone to bed and continued reading or watching short videos for some time before turning off the light. They said they had noticed that on those evenings they turned off the light later than they intended.

The participant said that after the previous visit they had become more aware of their phone use in bed. They said they had started leaving the phone to charge in the kitchen on some weeknights, and that on those nights they read a paper book in bed instead. They said they felt they fell asleep sooner on those nights and felt more rested in the morning. They had recorded these nights in the log with a note saying the phone was left in the kitchen. The coordinator recorded the participant's description in their own words and reminded the participant that the study observes normal habits and does not require any change.

The participant said that on weekend evenings they usually watched films with the other adult in the household and went to bed later. They said they did not usually use their phone in bed on weekends because they were already tired.

The participant explained what happened on the weekend away. They said they stayed with friends for two nights and did not take the log because they packed in a hurry. They said they used their phone in the evenings as usual and went to bed later than at home. They did not try to complete the missing pages from memory, and asked the coordinator how to record the missing weekend. The coordinator wrote a note on the log cover to record that the log was not available for that weekend, and the participant agreed with the note.

The participant said that remembering to complete the log each evening was the most difficult part of the study. They said that on some evenings they completed it the next morning instead. They asked whether evening reminders could be sent to help them complete the log at the right time. The coordinator noted this request.

The participant also asked whether a small travel version of the log could be provided, so that it could be packed easily for trips. The coordinator noted this request for the study team.

The participant completed the follow-up questionnaire on the tablet. The questionnaire asks about tiredness during the day, how rested the participant felt in the morning, and how they used screens in the evening. All questions were answered and the questionnaire was submitted without errors. The participant said some questions about the weekend away were harder to answer because they did not have the log.

The participant asked for the next visit to be scheduled early in the week, because their work is busier at the end of the week. The coordinator noted this preference.

No adverse events were reported. The participant had no questions about the study procedures beyond the requests above. The coordinator explained the next steps and confirmed that the participant knows how to reach the study team between visits.

At the end of the visit the participant received a new blank screen time log. The participant confirmed they understood how to continue completing the log.

Additional observations recorded during the visit: the participant said they felt well rested on the morning of the visit and that they had left their phone in the kitchen the night before. They said they had found it easier to wake up on mornings after nights without the phone in the bedroom. They asked whether this was a common observation among participants, and the coordinator explained that individual results and general findings are not discussed during the study.

The participant described their bedroom environment. They said the bedroom has thick curtains and is usually dark at night. They said they use an alarm clock rather than their phone alarm on nights when the phone is left in the kitchen. They said they bought the alarm clock during the period so that they could leave the phone outside the bedroom. The coordinator recorded this in the study notes.

The participant described their daytime screen use for context. They said they use a computer for most of the working day and that their eyes sometimes feel tired by the evening. They said they had started taking short breaks away from the screen at work, which they had read about in a magazine. The coordinator recorded this in the participant's own words.

The participant described how they completed the log. They said they kept the log on the bedside table with a pen, and filled it in before turning off the light on most nights. They said that on nights when they used the phone in bed, they sometimes forgot to complete the log because they fell asleep with the phone. They said they completed those entries the next morning and had marked them as completed in the morning.

The coordinator checked the log for any unclear entries. On three pages the bedtime was written in a way that could be read two ways, and the participant confirmed the correct times. The coordinator initialled each clarification and noted that it was given by the participant during the visit. No other entries needed clarification.

The participant asked how the log information would be used. The coordinator explained that the log is linked to the study code only and that the research team looks at general patterns across participants. The participant said they were reassured.

The participant asked how many more visits remained in the study. The coordinator explained the remaining schedule in general terms. The participant said they were happy to continue.

The participant suggested that the log could include a simple box to tick when the phone is left outside the bedroom, because they had been writing this as a note each time. The coordinator noted this suggestion for the study team.

Before leaving, the participant repeated the main instructions back to the coordinator: record evening screen use and bedtimes each night, mark any entries completed the next morning, and bring the log to the next visit. The coordinator confirmed that this was correct.

The coordinator noted the following for the study record: the screen time log was complete apart from one weekend away when the log was not available; the participant started leaving their phone in the kitchen on some weeknights and bought an alarm clock for this purpose; some entries were completed the following morning and were marked as such; and the participant requested evening reminders, a travel version of the log, a tick box for nights when the phone is left outside the bedroom, and appointments early in the week.

The visit ended around midday without any outstanding questions from the participant.

The coordinator also asked whether anything else had changed during the period. The participant said they had started going to bed at a more regular time on weeknights, especially on nights when the phone was left in the kitchen. They said that on weekends their bedtime was still later than on weekdays. They said they had recorded all bedtimes in the log, including the later weekend bedtimes, and had not changed anything on weekends.

The participant also mentioned that the other adult in the household had started leaving their own phone outside the bedroom on some nights, and that the bedroom felt quieter on those nights. The coordinator recorded this in the participant's own words.

## Observation 3: Community Activity Registry (STD-ACT-REG)

--- copy from here ---

Registry visit completed at the study site in the morning. The participant arrived by bus, on time, and confirmed their study code at reception. Consent was re-confirmed verbally at the start of the visit and the participant said they were happy to continue taking part in the registry. No changes to consent were requested.

The participant returned the paper activity record for the period since the last visit. The record asks the participant to note the main activities of each day, such as walking, gardening, housework, group activities and travel, and to tick whether they were mostly indoors or outdoors. The coordinator reviewed the record with the participant page by page. Entries were complete for every day. The participant had used the comments box to note days with unusual weather and a day when they had visitors at home.

The participant described their activity during the period. They said they had attended the community walking group every week. The group meets at the community centre and walks a flat route around the local park, at a gentle pace with a stop on a bench halfway round. The participant said they enjoyed the group and had made friends there. They said they had walked to the shops on most days and taken the bus for longer journeys, for example to visit family across town.

The participant said they had done gardening on dry days, including weeding, watering and some light pruning. They said they had not done any heavy digging during the period. They said that on wet days they stayed mostly indoors and did housework and some stretching exercises they had learned at the community centre.

The step counter was returned. The participant said they had worn it every day from getting up until going to bed and had charged it every night. The coordinator uploaded the step counter data during the visit and the upload was confirmed as successful by the study software. The study software showed recordings for every day of the period.

The participant said the clip on the step counter had become loose during the period and that they were worried it might fall off during the walking group or while gardening. They said it had not fallen off, but that they had checked it often during the day. The coordinator inspected the clip and confirmed that it was looser than when it was issued. The participant asked whether a new clip could be provided. The coordinator noted this request.

The participant said they had found the activity record easy to complete in the evening and that it had become part of their routine. They said they sometimes found it hard to decide whether a day was mostly indoors or mostly outdoors when they had spent time in both, and had ticked both boxes on those days. The coordinator explained that ticking both boxes is acceptable and recorded this in the study notes.

The participant said they would like information about other group activities run by the community centre, such as gentle exercise classes or social groups, because they had enjoyed the walking group. The coordinator noted this request and explained that the registry team does not organise these activities but could pass on general information provided by the community centre.

The participant completed the follow-up registry questionnaire on the tablet. All questions were answered and the questionnaire was submitted without errors. The participant said the questions about daily activities were easy to answer with the activity record beside them.

The participant said they had had no falls since the last visit. They said they hold the handrail when using the stairs at home and on the bus, and that they avoid walking on wet leaves in the park.

No falls or adverse events were reported. The participant had no questions about the registry procedures beyond the requests above. The coordinator explained the next steps and confirmed that the participant knows how to reach the registry team between visits.

At the end of the visit the participant received a new blank activity record and the charged step counter. The participant confirmed they understood how to continue.

Additional observations recorded during the visit: the participant said the bus had been on time and the walk from the bus stop to the study site had been pleasant. They said they had eaten breakfast before arriving and felt well. They asked for a glass of water during the visit, and the coordinator provided one.

The participant described the walking group in more detail. They said the group is led by a volunteer from the community centre and that the walk takes most of the morning, including a stop for conversation. They said the route is flat and has benches along the way. They said that on one occasion the walk was cancelled because of heavy rain, and that they had recorded this in the comments box.

The participant described their week in more detail. They said they visit a family member across town most weeks, travelling by bus and walking from the bus stop. They said they had a family visit at their own home on one weekend and spent more time indoors that weekend. They said they had walked around the garden with their visitors.

The participant described their gardening in more detail. They said they had been watering the garden in the early evening on warm days, which involves carrying a watering can from the outside tap. They said they had been pruning a hedge on dry mornings and that they wore the step counter while gardening, as instructed.

The coordinator checked the activity record for any unclear entries. On two pages the activity was written in a way that could be read two ways, and the participant clarified the activities. The coordinator initialled each clarification and noted that it was given by the participant during the visit. No other entries needed clarification.

The coordinator checked the step counter after the upload. Apart from the loose clip, the device showed no damage. The charging contacts were clean, the display worked normally and the battery indicator was normal. The step counter was paired again with the study tablet and the pairing was confirmed by the study software.

The participant asked how the registry information would be used. The coordinator explained that the information is linked to the study code only and that the registry team looks at general patterns across participants. The participant said they were reassured.

The participant suggested that the activity record could include a section for group activities, because they had been writing the walking group in the comments box each week. The coordinator noted this suggestion for the registry team.

Before leaving, the participant repeated the main instructions back to the coordinator: wear the step counter every day, charge it every night, complete the activity record each evening, and bring both to the next visit. The coordinator confirmed that this was correct.

The coordinator noted the following for the registry record: the activity record and step counter data were complete for the period; the participant attends the community walking group every week; the step counter clip has become loose; and the participant requested a new clip, information about other community centre group activities, and a section for group activities in future activity records.

The visit ended in the late morning without any outstanding questions from the participant.

The coordinator asked how the participant usually travelled to the community centre for the walking group. The participant said they usually walk there when the weather is dry and take the bus on wet days. They said the walk to the community centre takes them past the shops, so they often combine the walking group with their shopping on the same morning. They said they had recorded these combined mornings in the activity record as a single entry.

The participant also described the stretching exercises they do at home on wet days. They said the exercises were shown at a community centre session earlier in the year and that they do them in the living room while listening to the radio. They said they do not do them every day, but on most wet days. The coordinator recorded this in the participant's own words.

The participant said that the walking group volunteer had mentioned a possible change to the group's meeting day in the coming months. They said they would let the registry team know at the next visit if the change happens, because it might affect their weekly pattern.

## Observation 4: Home Meal Patterns Study (STD-DIET-07)

--- copy from here ---

Follow-up visit completed at the study site in the afternoon, as the participant had requested at the previous visit. The participant arrived on time and confirmed their study code at reception. Consent was re-confirmed verbally at the start of the visit and the participant said they were happy to continue taking part in the study. No changes to consent were requested.

The participant returned the paper meal diary for the period since the last visit. The diary asks the participant to record each meal and snack, the time, where it was eaten, who they ate with, and a short description of what was eaten. The coordinator reviewed the diary with the participant page by page. Breakfast, lunch and evening meal were recorded on most days. Snack entries were missing on several days.

The participant described their meal pattern during the period. They said they often skipped breakfast on busy working mornings, and instead had a hot drink before leaving home. They said that on those days they usually had an early lunch at work. They said they usually ate lunch at their desk on working days, and with colleagues in the staff room once or twice a week. They said they usually cooked an evening meal at home on weekdays and ate with the other adult in the household.

The participant said their weekend meals were less regular. They said they usually had a late breakfast on weekends, a light lunch, and a later evening meal, sometimes eaten out with family or friends. They said they had eaten out on two weekend evenings during the period and had recorded these meals in the diary the next morning.

The participant explained why snack entries were missing. They said they were unsure whether small items, such as a biscuit with a hot drink or a piece of fruit at their desk, counted as a snack that should be recorded. They said that on some days they recorded these items and on other days they did not. They asked for clearer instructions on what counts as a snack. The coordinator explained that any food eaten between meals should be recorded as a snack, however small, and noted the request for clearer written instructions for the study team.

The participant said they preferred completing the diary on paper rather than on the tablet. They said they had tried the tablet version for a few days at the start of the period but found it slower, and switched back to paper. The coordinator recorded that a few days at the start of the period were completed on the tablet and the rest on paper, and confirmed that the tablet entries had been received by the study software.

The participant said that keeping the diary had made them notice how often they skipped breakfast on working days. They said they had tried preparing breakfast the evening before on a few days so they could eat it on the bus. The coordinator recorded this observation in the participant's own words and reminded the participant that the study observes normal habits and does not require any change.

The participant completed the follow-up questionnaire on the tablet. The questionnaire asks about meal timing, eating with others, and how meals fit around work. All questions were answered and the questionnaire was submitted without errors. The participant said the questions were clear.

The participant asked for the next visit to be booked in the afternoon, because afternoon appointments are easier to arrange around their work. The coordinator noted this preference.

No adverse events were reported. The participant had no questions about the study procedures beyond the request for clearer snack instructions. The coordinator explained the next steps and confirmed that the participant knows how to reach the study team between visits.

At the end of the visit the participant received a new blank paper meal diary. The participant confirmed they understood how to continue, including recording all snacks however small.

Additional observations recorded during the visit: the participant said they had eaten lunch before the visit and felt well. They said they had come to the study site directly from work. They asked whether meals eaten while travelling should be recorded differently, and the coordinator confirmed that they should be recorded in the same way, with the place noted as travelling.

The participant described their working day in more detail. They said their work often starts early and that mornings are the busiest part of the day, which is why breakfast is often skipped. They said the afternoon is usually quieter, and that colleagues sometimes bring in food to share in the staff room. They said they had not always recorded these shared items because they were unsure whether they counted as snacks.

The participant described their evening routine. They said they usually cook with the other adult in the household and that they plan the evening meals for the week on Sunday. They said that on one weekday evening during the period they were too tired to cook and ate a simple meal instead, which they had recorded in the diary.

The participant described the meals eaten out on weekends. They said one was a family meal at a relative's home and the other was a meal with friends at a local restaurant. They said they had recorded both meals in the diary the next morning as best they could remember, and had marked them as completed the next morning.

The coordinator checked the diary for any unclear entries. On several pages the time of a meal had been left blank, and the participant gave approximate times where they could remember them. The coordinator initialled each addition and noted that it was given by the participant during the visit, and left the time blank where the participant could not remember. No other entries needed clarification.

The participant asked how the diary information would be used. The coordinator explained that the diary entries are linked to the study code only and that the research team looks at general patterns across participants. The participant said they were reassured.

The participant asked how many more visits remained in the study. The coordinator explained the remaining schedule in general terms. The participant said they were happy to continue.

The participant suggested that the diary could include a printed example of a completed day, showing how to record snacks and meals eaten out, because this would have answered their question about snacks at the start. The coordinator noted this suggestion for the study team.

The participant also said that a separate small section for drinks taken with snacks would make the diary easier to complete, because they were sometimes unsure whether to write the drink in the meal line or the snack line. The coordinator noted this suggestion.

Before leaving, the participant repeated the main instructions back to the coordinator: record every meal and snack with the time and place, record snacks however small, mark entries completed the next morning, and bring the diary to the next visit. The coordinator confirmed that this was correct.

The coordinator noted the following for the study record: breakfast, lunch and evening meal were recorded on most days; snack entries were missing on several days because the participant was unsure what counted as a snack; a few days at the start of the period were completed on the tablet and the rest on paper; meals eaten out on weekends were completed the next morning; and the participant requested clearer written snack instructions, a printed example of a completed day, a section for drinks with snacks, a paper diary, and afternoon appointments.

The visit ended in the late afternoon without any outstanding questions from the participant.

The coordinator also asked whether the participant's shopping routine had changed during the period. The participant said they usually do a large food shop on Sunday after planning the week's evening meals, and a small top-up shop during the week on the way home from work. They said they sometimes buy a snack for the journey home on top-up shopping days, and that they had not always recorded these snacks before this visit.

The participant said they would now record these journey snacks as well, following the coordinator's explanation that any food eaten between meals should be recorded.

## What to expect from the AI draft

- **Up to 5 summary points**, each stating one fact from the note (the AI chooses the most important facts from these long notes).
- **Up to 3 proposed follow-up actions**, administrative only, for example replacing a clip, sending reminders or booking the next visit.
- The reviewer can edit, remove or add actions, must add a comment to approve, or must give at least one reason to reject.
- Long notes take a few seconds longer to draft. If a draft fails with "invalid output" or "ungrounded output", click **Retry** once.
