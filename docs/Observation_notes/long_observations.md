## Sleep study, note 1: Baseline enrolment visit

--- copy from here ---

Baseline visit completed at the study site in the morning. The participant arrived on time and confirmed their study code at reception. The coordinator explained the purpose of the visit again and the participant said they had read the information sheet before arriving and had no further questions about it. Consent was re-confirmed verbally at the start of the visit and the participant said they were comfortable continuing with the study. No changes to consent were requested.

The coordinator went through the sleep diary with the participant page by page. The diary asks for bedtime, the time the participant thinks they fell asleep, any awakenings during the night, the final wake time in the morning, the time they got out of bed, and a short rating of how rested they felt. The participant practised filling in one example page for the previous night while the coordinator watched. They completed every field correctly on the first attempt. They asked whether they should record a short awakening if they were not sure how long it lasted, and the coordinator explained that they should write their best estimate and add a short comment in the notes box. The participant said this was clear.

The participant described their usual sleep routine. On weekdays they usually go to bed in the late evening after reading for a while, and they usually get up early for work. On weekends they said they go to bed later and sleep in, sometimes by more than an hour. They said they often wake once during the night and usually fall asleep again quickly. They described the bedroom as quiet most of the time, with some traffic noise on weekend nights from a nearby road. They said they keep the curtains closed and use a fan during warm weather. They reported drinking tea in the afternoon on most days and said they rarely drink coffee after lunch. They said they do not usually take naps during the day, but sometimes rest on the sofa on Sunday afternoons.

The wearable sleep device was issued during the visit. The coordinator showed the participant how to put the device on, how to check that it is recording, and how to charge it. The participant practised fastening and removing the strap and confirmed it felt comfortable on the wrist. The device was paired with the study tablet and the pairing was confirmed by the study software before the participant left. The coordinator explained that the device should be worn every night and charged each morning, and that it does not need to be worn during the day. The participant asked whether the device could get wet, and the coordinator explained that it should be removed before showering. The participant said they would keep the charger next to their bed.

The participant completed the baseline sleep questionnaire on the tablet. All questions were answered and the questionnaire was submitted without errors. The participant said some of the questions about the previous month were difficult to answer precisely, and they gave their best estimates. They said the questionnaire took a short time to complete and the wording was clear.

The coordinator asked about the participant's work schedule, because the study needs to know about regular changes to sleep times. The participant said they work mostly daytime hours with occasional late finishes once or twice a month. They did not expect any travel in the coming weeks but said a family event was planned at the end of the month that might mean a late night. The coordinator asked them to record any unusual nights in the diary notes box, and the participant agreed.

The participant asked how the information from the device would be used. The coordinator explained that the device records movement and timing patterns during the night, that the data is linked to the study code only, and that the research team does not receive names or contact details through the device. The participant said they were reassured by this explanation. They also asked how long each visit would take in the future, and the coordinator explained that follow-up visits are usually shorter than the baseline visit.

The participant mentioned that they sometimes find it hard to remember tasks in the evening when they are tired, and asked whether reminders could help them complete the diary each night. The coordinator noted this request. The participant also said that morning appointments suit them best because they can attend before work, and they asked for future visits to be in the morning where possible. The coordinator noted this preference.

No adverse events were reported during the visit. The participant did not report any discomfort from the device strap during the short practice period. The participant had no other questions about the study procedures. The coordinator explained the next steps in the study, including the date range for the first follow-up visit, and confirmed that the participant knows how to reach the study team between visits if they have questions about the device or the diary.

At the end of the visit the participant received the paper sleep diary for the next period, the charged wearable device, the charging cable and a printed instruction card showing how to wear and charge the device. The participant confirmed they understood how to complete the diary each morning and how to charge the device each day. The participant left the study site in the late morning.

Additional observations recorded during the visit: the participant arrived carrying a bottle of water and said they had eaten breakfast before arriving. They said they had slept as usual the night before the visit and did not feel especially tired. They asked to sit near the window during the questionnaire because the light helped them read the tablet screen, and the coordinator moved the chair. The participant mentioned that they share their home with one other adult and that the other adult usually goes to bed later than they do. They said this sometimes means they are woken by noise when the other adult comes to bed, but that it does not happen every night. They said their bedroom is cool in the evening and warm in the early morning when the sun comes up, and that they sometimes wake early in summer because of the light. They said they use an alarm clock on weekdays and do not use one on weekends.

The participant also described their evening routine in more detail. They said they usually eat their evening meal early, watch some television, and then read in bed for a while before turning off the light. They said they sometimes use their phone in bed to check messages, and they asked whether this would affect the study. The coordinator explained that the study observes normal habits and that the participant does not need to change their routine, but asked them to note anything unusual in the diary. The participant said they would try to remember to do this.

The coordinator checked the device settings once more before the participant left, confirmed the battery was fully charged, and confirmed that the participant's study code was shown correctly on the study tablet. The participant signed the device loan form and received a copy. The diary pages were numbered and the coordinator explained which page to start on that evening. The participant repeated the main instructions back to the coordinator to confirm they had understood them: complete the diary each morning, wear the device every night, charge it every morning, and bring both the diary and the device to the next visit. The coordinator confirmed that this was correct.

Before leaving, the participant asked whether they could contact the study team if the device stopped working, and the coordinator confirmed that they could and showed them where the study team contact details are printed on the instruction card. The participant said they felt prepared for the first period of the study. The visit ended without any outstanding questions from the participant.

The coordinator noted that the participant was attentive throughout the visit, asked clear questions, and completed every practice task correctly. The participant said they were interested in learning more about their own sleep patterns, and the coordinator explained that individual results are not shared during the study but that a general summary of the study findings may be made available at the end. The participant said they understood this.

They said they fell asleep fairly quickly on weeknights and woke once during the night on most nights, usually falling asleep again soon afterwards. They said weekend nights were later and less regular, and that on one weekend they stayed up late for a family gathering and slept in the next morning. They rated themselves as feeling reasonably rested on most mornings and less rested on the mornings after the late nights.

The wearable sleep device was returned in good condition. The participant said they had worn it every night and charged it every morning as instructed. The strap was clean and secure. The participant said they had become used to wearing the device after the first few nights and that it no longer disturbed them.

They also said the evening reminder idea mentioned at baseline would still be helpful, because on two mornings they had nearly forgotten to fill in the diary and completed it later in the day.

No adverse events were reported. The participant said the device strap had not caused any skin irritation and that they had no discomfort during the night. The participant had no questions about the study procedures beyond the requests above.

The coordinator explained the next steps and the date range for the next follow-up visit. The participant received a new blank sleep diary for the next period and the cleaned and charged wearable device. The participant confirmed they understood how to continue with the diary and device as before.

Additional observations recorded during the visit: the participant arrived on foot and said the walk to the study site had taken less time than expected. They said they had slept well the night before the visit. They asked whether the information about their phone use in bed would be included in the study data, and the coordinator explained that the diary notes are part of the study record and are linked to the study code only. The participant said this was fine.

The participant described the bedroom environment during the period. They said the weather had been mild and that they had not needed the fan on most nights. They said the traffic noise on weekend nights had been similar to usual. They mentioned that the other adult in the household had been away for several nights during the period and that on those nights they were not woken by noise when the other adult came to bed. They said they felt they slept more continuously on those nights, and they had written this in the diary notes box.

The participant also described their daytime routine. They said they had continued to drink tea in the afternoon and had not changed this. They said they had not taken any naps on weekdays, but had rested on the sofa on two weekend afternoons without falling asleep. They said they had been more physically active than usual on one weekend because of gardening, and that they felt they fell asleep more quickly that night.

The coordinator checked the diary for any unclear entries. One entry had a wake time written in a way that could be read two ways, and the participant clarified the correct time. The coordinator added a short note beside the entry, initialled it, and recorded that the clarification was given by the participant during the visit. No other entries needed clarification.

The coordinator also checked the device for any damage. The charging contacts were clean, the screen showed no scratches and the clasp closed firmly. The battery indicator showed full charge after a short top-up during the visit. The device was paired again with the study tablet to confirm the connection was still working, and the pairing was confirmed by the study software.

The participant asked how many more visits were planned in the study. The coordinator explained the remaining schedule in general terms, including the mid-study visit and the close-out visit. The participant said the schedule was manageable and that they were happy to continue.

The participant said they had found the instruction card helpful and had kept it next to the bed. They suggested that a small checklist on the back of the diary might help them remember the daily steps, and the coordinator noted this suggestion for the study team.

At the end of the visit the participant confirmed they had everything they needed for the next period: the new diary, the device, the charging cable and the instruction card. The visit ended in the late morning with no outstanding questions from the participant.

The coordinator noted that the participant had followed all study instructions during the period, that the diary and the device data were complete, and that the participant remained engaged and positive about taking part. The coordinator also noted the participant's three requests from this visit: a second charger for use at work, evening reminders to complete the diary, and future appointments in the morning early in the week.

The coordinator asked the participant how they had found completing the diary each morning. The participant said it usually took a few minutes and that they now completed it while having breakfast. They said the hardest field to complete was the number of awakenings, because they were not always sure whether they had woken briefly or had only been dreaming. The coordinator reminded the participant to record their best estimate and to use the notes box when they were unsure, as explained at the baseline visit. The participant said they would continue to do this.

The participant also mentioned that on two evenings they had fallen asleep on the sofa before going to bed and had recorded this in the notes box. They said they were not sure whether to record the sofa sleep as their bedtime, and had written the time they moved to bed as the bedtime with a note about the sofa. The coordinator confirmed this was a sensible way to record it and suggested continuing in the same way.

Follow-up visit completed at the study site in the morning. The participant arrived slightly late and explained that public transport had been delayed. They confirmed their study code at reception. They said they had slept more than usual during those days and had stayed in bed later. The coordinator recorded the participant's account in the diary notes and did not ask the participant to complete the pages from memory.

The wearable sleep device was returned, but the participant said it had not been worn on several nights. The coordinator uploaded the device data during the visit and the upload was confirmed as successful by the study software. The strap was in good condition and the device showed no damage. The participant said the device had been comfortable during the nights it was worn. The participant completed the follow-up sleep questionnaire on the tablet. All questions were answered and the questionnaire was submitted without errors. The participant said some questions about the previous weeks were harder to answer because the travel and the cold had changed their usual routine.

They said sleep while travelling was less regular because they were sleeping in a different bed and the room was warmer than at home. They said they went to bed later during the trip and woke earlier because of noise in the house where they were staying. They said that during the two days with a cold they slept for longer and felt less rested.

The participant said they would like a spare charger so that the device can still be charged when they travel. They said another trip is planned in the coming months. The participant repeated that evening reminders to complete the diary would help, and said that the missing entries during the trip might not have happened with reminders. The coordinator noted this.

They did not report any discomfort from the device. The participant had no further questions about the study procedures.

They said they could not remember the number of awakenings on those nights with confidence and preferred not to guess. They said they did not try to borrow a charger because they did not know whether a different charger would be safe for the device. The coordinator confirmed that only the study charger should be used and thanked the participant for not using a different one.

The coordinator checked the device after the upload and found that the battery health indicator was normal. The device was charged to full during the visit and paired again with the study tablet. The pairing was confirmed by the study software.

The participant asked whether the study team could send a reminder before future trips to pack the charger. The coordinator noted this suggestion along with the request for a spare charger. The participant said that a spare charger kept in their travel bag would be the simplest solution.

The participant said that apart from the trip and the cold, the period had been similar to the previous one. They said they were still reading in bed before sleep and still using their phone in bed on some nights, and that they had continued to note this in the diary when they remembered.

The coordinator explained that the next visit would include the mid-study questionnaire in addition to the usual diary and device steps, and that this questionnaire is slightly longer. The participant said this was fine and asked for the visit to be booked in the morning again.

At the end of the visit the participant confirmed they had the new diary, the device, the charging cable and the instruction card. They said they would keep the charger in the same place at home and pack it in their bag for any future trip. The visit ended without any outstanding questions from the participant.

The coordinator noted the following for the study record: the diary had incomplete entries for the travel nights and two blank pages for the days with a cold; the device was not worn on the travel nights after the battery ran out; the participant requested a spare charger for travel, a smaller travel diary format and evening reminders; and the participant preferred morning appointments.

The coordinator also asked whether anything else had changed during the period. The participant said nothing else had changed and that their work hours had been the same as before. They said they were looking forward to returning to their usual routine now that the trip was over and they had recovered from the cold.

## Sleep study, note 4: Mid-study visit with a schedule change

Mid-study visit completed at the study site in the morning. The participant arrived on time and confirmed their study code. Consent was re-confirmed verbally at the start of the visit and the participant said they were happy to continue taking part. No changes to consent were requested.

The participant explained at the start of the visit that their working pattern had changed since the last visit. They had moved to a rotating schedule that includes some early starts and some late finishes each week. They said the new schedule started a few weeks before the visit and is expected to continue for the rest of the study period. The coordinator noted this change because it affects the participant's sleep times.

The participant returned the paper sleep diary for the period. All pages were present. The coordinator reviewed the diary with the participant page by page. Entries were complete for every night, and the participant had used the notes box to mark early-start days and late-finish days. On early-start days the participant went to bed earlier than before and got up much earlier. On late-finish days they went to bed later and slept in when possible. The participant said they had found it helpful to mark the work pattern in the diary so the coordinator could see the reason for the changes.

The participant described their sleep during the period. They said that on nights before early starts they sometimes found it harder to fall asleep, because they were going to bed earlier than their body was used to. They said they often woke before the alarm on those mornings. They said that after late finishes they fell asleep quickly but woke during the night more often than usual. They rated themselves as feeling less rested on mornings after late finishes and after the first early start of each week.

The participant said they had started having a short nap in the early afternoon on some days off, because they felt tired after a run of early starts. They said the naps were usually short and they set an alarm so they did not sleep too long. They had recorded the naps in the daytime section of the diary. The coordinator thanked the participant for recording the naps.

The participant said they had started drinking coffee in the morning on early-start days, which they did not do before. They said they still drink tea in the afternoon. They had not changed anything else about their evening routine, apart from going to bed at different times depending on the shift.

The wearable sleep device was returned in good condition. The participant said they had worn it every night and charged it every morning, including on early-start days when they charged it while getting ready for work. The coordinator uploaded the device data and the upload was confirmed as successful by the study software. The study software showed recordings for every night of the period.

The coordinator explained that the study records what participants do in their normal lives and that the diary notes help the study team understand the data. The participant said they would continue to record these details.

At the end of the visit the participant confirmed they had the new diary, the device, the charging cable, the spare charger at work and the instruction card. The visit ended in the late morning without any outstanding questions from the participant.

The coordinator noted the following for the study record: the participant moved to a rotating work schedule with early starts and late finishes; the participant began taking short naps on some days off and using a sleep mask on early-start nights; the device strap shows light wear at the clasp and may need replacing; and the participant requested that future visits are booked on a day off after checking their schedule.

The coordinator asked the participant how they planned the diary on mornings with an early start. The participant said they now completed the diary on the bus to work on early-start days, using the small pen kept inside the diary cover, and completed it at breakfast on other days. They said this routine had worked well and that the evening reminders helped them remember to keep the diary and the pen in their bag.

The participant also said that on two nights during the period they had swapped shifts with a colleague at short notice, so their bedtime changed later in the day than planned. They had recorded both swaps in the notes box, and the coordinator confirmed that the entries were clear.
